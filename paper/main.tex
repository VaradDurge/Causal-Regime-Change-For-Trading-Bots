\begin{abstract}
Regime-aware reinforcement-learning (RL) trading agents are usually judged by back-tested returns, which cannot show whether the regime signal drives their decisions. We couple a Wasserstein regime-identity tracker with three interventions on the regime input, and we train a proximal policy optimisation (PPO) twin that differs only by lacking that input. On BTC/USDT four-hour bars the tracker lowers the mean template-matching cost by a factor of 4.9, but it issues no bull label after burn-in. On 11{,}430 out-of-fold bars ablation moves the conditioned position on 60.7\% of bars and the agent de-risks in bear and crisis segments, yet only 0.1\% of bars pass all three checks, against 33.5\% for an archived moment-sorted labelling. The Sharpe ratios are $-0.16$ for the conditioned agent, $-1.12$ for the blind twin and $0.35$ for buy-and-hold. Those four-hour numbers are the published single run. A five-seed repetition of the same recipe is reported beside them and does not replace them.

The comparison in this paper is five seeds of timeframe-native twins on BTC/USDT and ETH/USDT, at 15\,min and 1\,h. A one-factor grid on the validation split did not meet the pre-registered bar: both twins with position standard deviation above 0.05, a share of bars with $|a|>0.95$ below 0.80, and ablation of at least 0.10, on all four tapes. The baseline had the lowest mean deficit (about 0.985), so \texttt{configs/final.yaml} stayed the native recipe: raw reward, scale 100, entropy coefficient 0.01, uniform 2{,}000-bar windows, tracker v1, no perturbation and no faithfulness regularizer. Holdout and live were scored once and were not used to choose the recipe. The mean share of bars with $|a|>0.95$ is 97--100\% for every twin, and the mean holdout Sharpe ratio is negative for both twins. The conditioned agent beats the blind twin on a majority of seeds in two of eight windows only. On BTC 1\,h holdout its mean return is $+2.8\%$ (standard deviation 38.2) against $-26.1\%$ (12.8), with beats share 0.80 and ablation 7.3\% (13.6). On ETH 1\,h live the means are $+0.4\%$ against $-5.5\%$, with beats share 0.80. It does not win overall. The agents are not regularized, and both verifiers report small faithfulness rates.

Repeating the four-hour walk-forward study on seeds 42--46 does not turn that single run into a stable gain. Tracker v1's conditioned mean return is $+57.8\%$ only because seed 46 returns $+515\%$. The other four seeds are negative, and the mean Sharpe ratio is $-0.27$. Tracker v2's mean return is $-83.6\%$, and the moment-sorted labelling's is $-44.0\%$. Legacy faithfulness stays under 3\% for both trackers and is 12.5\% for moment-sorted names. On the native validation twins the raw Gaussian mean lies outside $[-1,1]$ on 95.6--99.8\% of bars, which is what the clip then executes as a rail. A tanh execution and a five-point discrete action were screened on the validation split only. Neither met the pre-registered bar on all four tapes, so holdout and live were not scored for those modes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%======================================================================
Cryptocurrency markets move through very different conditions within a few years. Bitcoin rose sharply in 2021, lost roughly three quarters of its value between late 2021 and late 2022, and then spent 2023--2026 alternating between recoveries and drawdowns. A trading policy fitted to such a history without any notion of market state tends to learn one averaged behaviour that suits none of these phases. A natural remedy is to detect the prevailing regime and hand it to the policy as an extra input. Ndoutoumou \emph{et al.} combine a hidden Markov model with reinforcement-learning portfolio agents and report stronger risk-adjusted returns than the same agents without the regime signal~\cite{ndoutoumou2025hmm}. RegimeFolio builds sector portfolios around detected regimes in non-stationary markets~\cite{zhang2025regimefolio}, and explainable classifiers have been used to allocate assets by market state~\cite{zhang2020regimealloc}.

What this work rarely checks is whether the regime input matters. A regime-conditioned agent can report that it ``cut exposure because a crisis was detected'' while its actual computation ignores the crisis probability, or responds to it in the wrong direction. An explanation that is easy to understand is not necessarily faithful to the computation that produced the decision~\cite{mamalakis2025enigma}. Reviews of explainable machine learning find that explanations are often judged by whether users trust them, while standard tests of whether an explanation is true of the model are still missing~\cite{wiratsin2025xai}. Finance relies on much the same post-hoc tools, and their evaluation is uneven~\cite{yeo2025financial}. In high-stakes settings, the duty to explain a decision is not optional~\cite{reinhardt2025xai}.

A second, quieter problem sits inside the regime detector. An HMM refitted on new data returns its states in an arbitrary order, so ``state~2'' may mean crisis in one window and calm in the next. Sorting states by their moments after every fit repairs the names locally, but it forces four names onto whatever four components the latest fit produces, even if one of them no longer resembles anything seen before. We instead keep persistent bull, bear, calm and crisis templates and match each new component to the nearest template by the closed-form 2-Wasserstein distance, so a name survives a refit only when the component still resembles it.

This paper asks two linked questions. Does Wasserstein tracking give regime labels that keep their meaning across walk-forward refits? And when an RL agent is conditioned on those labels, does its behaviour depend on them in the way the labels' names imply? A four-hour walk-forward study on BTC/USDT answers both. The trading comparison for 15-min and 1-h candles is a five-seed study of timeframe-native twins on BTC/USDT and ETH/USDT, and that comparison is the result reported here. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A walk-forward implementation of Wasserstein template tracking for a four-state Gaussian HMM, with the closed-form diagonal $\Wtwo$ distance, probability aggregation and template smoothing written out as equations that match the released code.
  \item A per-decision verification suite of three interventions on the regime input (ablation, counterfactual injection and a sensitivity sweep), validated on reference policies that are faithful, regime-ignoring and inverted by construction.
  \item A twin experiment in which regime-conditioned and regime-blind PPO agents share architecture, seed and training budget, evaluated walk-forward with bootstrapped confidence intervals and a per-regime breakdown.
  \item An empirical finding: replacing moment sorting with Wasserstein tracking leaves the regime input load-bearing but collapses the faithfulness rate from 33.5\% to 0.1\%. We trace this to template starvation and to counterfactual inputs that lie outside the training distribution.
  \item A live paper-trading console that runs both twins on closed Binance candles, and a five-seed study of timeframe-native twins on BTC/USDT and ETH/USDT at 15\,min and 1\,h. The validation grid did not meet the pre-registered bar, so the native recipe was frozen before holdout and live were scored. The conditioned twin beats the blind twin on a majority of seeds in two of eight windows only. Every twin spends 97--100\% of bars at $|a|>0.95$, and the mean holdout Sharpe ratio is negative for both twins.
  \item A five-seed repetition of the four-hour study, and a check of whether Gaussian clipping puts the native twins on the rails. The four-hour return mean is one seed. The raw policy mean lies outside $[-1,1]$ on nearly every validation bar, and neither a tanh execution nor a five-point discrete action passed the pre-registered bar on every tape.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
%======================================================================
\subsection{Regime detection}
Ndoutoumou, Yin and Cheng fit a Gaussian HMM and pass the inferred regime into reinforcement-learning agents on a Dow Jones portfolio. For several algorithms the hybrid agent improves the Sharpe and Sortino ratios relative to the same agent without the HMM~\cite{ndoutoumou2025hmm}. The comparison is on back-tested returns. It does not test whether the learned policy actually depends on the regime input. Tsang and Chen detect regime change in the foreign-exchange market with directional-change indicators, and use the detector to chart the period around Brexit~\cite{tsang2018regime}. Zhang, Yi and Chen use explainable machine learning to classify regimes and to support asset allocation~\cite{zhang2020regimealloc}. RegimeFolio is a regime-aware learning system for sectoral portfolio optimisation when markets are non-stationary~\cite{zhang2025regimefolio}. Standard HMMs switch state too readily on noisy data; we suppress this with a simple minimum-dwell rule, and we keep regime names stable across refits by Wasserstein template matching, described in Section~\ref{sec:method}.

\subsection{Reinforcement learning for portfolio optimisation}
Deep reinforcement learning is now a standard way to treat portfolio choice as a sequential decision. Surveys cover the single-agent literature and the move toward multi-agent systems~\cite{mortaji2024review,rout2026review}. Wang, Jin and Li cast asset-weight management as a Markov decision process and train a deep RL agent for diversification~\cite{wang2022drl}. Tamuly, Bhutani and Sukriti apply deep RL to dynamic capital allocation~\cite{tamuly2024drl}. Naik \emph{et al.} use proximal policy optimisation and deep deterministic policy gradient for real-time rebalancing on Indian equities, with the Sharpe ratio as the risk-adjusted objective~\cite{naik2025realtime}. Huang and Jing adapt a single-stock portfolio with a deep deterministic policy gradient agent~\cite{huang2025singlestock}. Gao and Tang evaluate advantage actor-critic and proximal policy optimisation on A-share portfolios drawn from CSI~300 constituents, under A-share trading constraints~\cite{gao2025drl}. Shrirao \emph{et al.} train portfolio policies intended to remain usable under adversarial market conditions~\cite{shrirao2025adversarial}. Across this work, a higher Sharpe ratio is treated as evidence that the agent has adapted. None of it checks whether a named regime input is what the policy uses.

\subsection{Explainability}
Mamalakis \emph{et al.} separate an explanation that is faithful to a deep network from one that is merely comprehensible~\cite{mamalakis2025enigma}. Wiratsin and Ragkhitwetsagul review whether explainable-AI techniques improve human trust, and they note the lack of standard evaluation metrics~\cite{wiratsin2025xai}. Reinhardt and Buchholz argue that explainability in high-stakes domains is an obligation, not only a convenience~\cite{reinhardt2025xai}. Yeo \emph{et al.} survey how post-hoc explanations are used in finance and how their limits affect decisions~\cite{yeo2025financial}. Our tests are different in kind: they intervene on one named input of a sequential policy rather than attributing a static prediction.

\subsection{Causal inference in financial decisions}
Qiu \emph{et al.} build a causal-inference model for stock-trend forecasting, on the view that predictive accuracy alone does not identify which dependencies the forecast uses~\cite{qiu2025cistf}. Katamaneni \emph{et al.} study machine-learning causal inference for decisions that must be revised as the environment changes~\cite{katamaneni2025causal}. Both treat causality as a property of the market time series. The interventions in this paper are different. The policy is a known function, so setting its regime input to a chosen value measures the effect of that input on the action, not a causal effect in the market.

\section{Preliminaries}
%======================================================================
\subsection{Gaussian hidden Markov model}
Let $x_t\in\R^3$ be the regime features at bar $t$. A $K$-state Gaussian HMM has latent states $z_t\in\{1,\dots,K\}$, a transition matrix $A_{ij}=P(z_{t+1}=j\mid z_t=i)$, initial probabilities $\pi_0$ and emissions $x_t\mid z_t=k\sim\mathcal N(m_k,\Sigma_k)$. We use diagonal $\Sigma_k=\mathrm{diag}(v_k)$. Parameters are fitted by expectation--maximisation. The posterior $\gamma_t(k)=P(z_t=k\mid x_{1:n})$ comes from the forward--backward recursion and the most likely path from Viterbi decoding. The likelihood is invariant to permuting the state indices, so two fits on overlapping data can return the same regimes under different indices.

\subsection{2-Wasserstein distance between Gaussians}
For probability measures $\mu,\nu$ on $\R^d$ with finite second moments,
\begin{equation}
\Wtwo^2(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)}\int\lVert x-y\rVert^2\,d\gamma(x,y),
\end{equation}
where $\Gamma(\mu,\nu)$ is the set of couplings with marginals $\mu$ and $\nu$. For Gaussians the infimum has the closed form
\begin{multline}
\Wtwo^2\bigl(\mathcal N(m_1,\Sigma_1),\mathcal N(m_2,\Sigma_2)\bigr)=\lVert m_1-m_2\rVert^2\\
+\operatorname{tr}\!\Bigl(\Sigma_1+\Sigma_2-2\bigl(\Sigma_2^{1/2}\Sigma_1\Sigma_2^{1/2}\bigr)^{1/2}\Bigr).
\end{multline}
With diagonal covariances $\Sigma_i=\mathrm{diag}(s_i^2)$ the matrices commute and the trace term reduces to $\lVert s_1-s_2\rVert^2$, so
\begin{equation}
\Wtwo^2=\lVert m_1-m_2\rVert^2+\lVert s_1-s_2\rVert^2 .
\label{eq:w2diag}
\end{equation}
It compares location and spread on the scale of the features and costs $O(d)$, so no numerical optimal-transport solver is needed.

\subsection{Interventions on a policy input}
A trained deterministic policy $a=\pi_\theta(o)$ is a known function of its observation. Write $o=(u,p)$, where $p$ is the regime-probability block. We use $\pi_\theta(u,\mathrm{do}(p{=}p'))$ for the action obtained when $p$ is set to $p'$ and everything else is held fixed. Because $\pi_\theta$ is available in closed form, this intervention needs no causal identification assumptions. It measures the effect of the regime input on the policy's output, not any causal effect in the market. A regime signal is \emph{load-bearing} if such interventions change the action. It is \emph{faithful to its names} if the change has the sign that the names imply.

\subsection{Data and causal features}
Let $C_t$ be the close of bar $t$. The features are the log return $r_t=\log(C_t/C_{t-1})$, the 20-bar rolling standard deviation $\sigma_t$ of $r$, the trend $\tau_t=C_t/\mathrm{SMA}_{20}(C)_t-1$, the 14-bar Wilder RSI scaled to $[0,1]$, and the MACD histogram (EMA$_{12}-$EMA$_{26}$ minus its 9-bar signal) divided by $C_t$. Each feature at $t$ uses rows $\le t$ only. A unit test checks that features computed on a truncated history equal the prefix of the full computation. The regime model uses $x_t=(r_t,\sigma_t,\tau_t)$. The agent sees all five as $f_t$, rescaled to unit order by a fixed map $\phi$ ($r,\sigma,\text{MACD}\times100$; $\tau\times10$; RSI$\mapsto 2\,\text{RSI}-1$).

\subsection{Walk-forward HMM refits}
The tracker makes a single forward pass over the series (Fig.~\ref{fig:timeline}). The first $B=252$ bars (six weeks) form a burn-in. After that, refit $j$ starts at $t_j=B+jS$ with stride $S=42$ bars (one week). At each refit a four-state diagonal HMM is fitted by EM (200 iterations, seed 42) to the rows strictly before $t_j$, standardised with that window's mean $\bar x$ and standard deviation $s$. The model then decodes the window $W_j=\{t_j,\dots,t_j+S-1\}$. Component parameters are mapped back to feature units so that different fits can be compared:
\begin{equation}
\mu_k=\hat m_k\odot s+\bar x,\qquad \sigma_k=\sqrt{\hat v_k}\odot s .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Template initialisation}
The burn-in fit is the only place where names come from moments. The rule $\ell$ sorts components by mean return. Of the two lowest, the one with lower volatility is \emph{bear} and the other is \emph{crisis}. Of the two highest, the one with lower volatility is \emph{calm} and the other is \emph{bull}. This yields templates $(\bar\mu_g,\bar\sigma_g)$ for $g\in\G=\{\text{bull},\text{bear},\text{calm},\text{crisis}\}$.

\subsection{Wasserstein identity matching}
At refit $j$ the cost of naming component $k$ as regime $g$ is the diagonal Wasserstein distance~\eqref{eq:w2diag}:
\begin{equation}
D_{kg}=\lVert\mu_k-\bar\mu_g\rVert^2+\lVert\sigma_k-\bar\sigma_g\rVert^2 ,
\end{equation}
and every component takes its nearest template independently,
\begin{equation}
\pi_j(k)=\argmin_{g\in\G} D_{kg}.
\label{eq:match}
\end{equation}
The matching need not be one-to-one: two components may share a template, and a template may receive none. The regime probabilities for $t\in W_j$ are the summed posteriors
\begin{equation}
p_t(g)=\sum_{k:\,\pi_j(k)=g}\gamma_t(k),
\label{eq:agg}
\end{equation}
and an unmatched template has $p_t(g)=0$.

\subsection{Template smoothing}
After window $W_j$ is labelled, every matched template moves a step $\eta=0.2$ toward its members. Let $w_k=\sum_{t\in W_j}\gamma_t(k)$ and $\omega_k=w_k/\sum_{k'\in M_g}w_{k'}$ for the member set $M_g=\{k:\pi_j(k)=g\}$. Then
\begin{equation}
\bar\mu_g\leftarrow(1-\eta)\,\bar\mu_g+\eta\sum_{k\in M_g}\omega_k\mu_k ,
\label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
and likewise for $\bar\sigma_g$. A template with $M_g=\emptyset$ is left unchanged. The update uses window $W_j$ and affects only windows after it.

\subsection{Hard labels and dwell constraint}
The hard label $\hat h_t$ is the name of the Viterbi state, $\pi_j(\hat z_t)$. Runs shorter than $L=30$ bars (five days) are merged into the preceding label, with the last issued label carried across window boundaries. Hard labels are used only for reporting and per-regime evaluation. The agent sees the probabilities $p_t$.

\subsection{Twin agents}
\label{sec:twins}
The trading environment is a Gymnasium episode over the training rows. At bar $t$ the agent chooses a target position $a_t\in[-1,1]$ and receives
\begin{equation}
R_t=a_t\,r_{t+1}-c\,\lvert a_t-a_{t-1}\rvert,\qquad c=10^{-3},
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
that is, the next log return less a 10-basis-point turnover cost. The conditioned agent observes $o_t=[\phi(f_t),a_{t-1},p_t]\in\R^{10}$ and the blind twin observes $[\phi(f_t),a_{t-1}]\in\R^{6}$. Both are PPO agents from Stable-Baselines3 with identical settings (Table~\ref{tab:hyper}). The only architectural difference is four extra input weights in the first layer, and that difference is the experiment.

\subsection{Verification suite}
For each out-of-fold bar we freeze the conditioned policy, the market features and the previous position, and intervene on $p$ alone. Let $a_t=\pi_\theta(u_t,p_t)$ be the realised action, $e_g$ the one-hot vector of regime $g$, and $\rho$ a defensive rank with $\rho(\text{bull})=0<\rho(\text{calm})=1<\rho(\text{bear})=2<\rho(\text{crisis})=3$.

\emph{Ablation.} The regime input is load-bearing at $t$ if
\begin{equation}
\Delta^{\text{ab}}_t=\bigl\lvert a_t-\pi_\theta(u_t,\mathrm{do}(p{=}\mathbf 0))\bigr\rvert\ \ge\ \delta_{\text{ab}}=0.15 .
\end{equation}

\emph{Counterfactual.} With $g_t=\argmax_g p_t(g)$, forcing crisis must cut the position and forcing bull must raise it, each by at least $\delta_{\text{cf}}=0.10$, whenever the forced regime differs in rank from $g_t$:
\begin{align}
\rho(g_t)<3 &\Rightarrow a_t-\pi_\theta(u_t,\mathrm{do}(p{=}e_{\text{crisis}}))\ge\delta_{\text{cf}},\\
\rho(g_t)>0 &\Rightarrow \pi_\theta(u_t,\mathrm{do}(p{=}e_{\text{bull}}))-a_t\ge\delta_{\text{cf}}.
\end{align}

\emph{Sensitivity.} For $q\in\{0,0.1,\dots,1\}$ we inject a crisis probability $q$ and rescale the other entries:
\begin{equation}
p^{(q)}=(1-q)\,\frac{p_t^{\setminus c}}{\lVert p_t^{\setminus c}\rVert_1}+q\,e_{\text{crisis}},
\end{equation}
where $p_t^{\setminus c}$ is $p_t$ with its crisis entry set to zero (uniform over the other three if that leaves nothing). The curve $y_i=\pi_\theta(u_t,\mathrm{do}(p{=}p^{(q_i)}))$ passes if it rises at most once, $\sum_i\mathbb 1[y_{i+1}>y_i+10^{-8}]\le 1$.

A bar is \emph{faithful} when all three checks pass. The faithfulness rate $F$ is the share of faithful bars. Each bar costs 15 batched forward passes.

\subsection{Live paper-trading console}
\label{sec:console}
The repository also includes an interactive console, built with Streamlit, in which both twins trade BTC/USDT with paper money as Binance candles close (Fig.~\ref{fig:console}). It never sends an order. The user chooses 15-min, 1-h or 4-h candles, as on a charting terminal, and every time stamp is shown in Indian Standard Time (IST, UTC+5:30).

\emph{Market feed.} Candles come from Binance's public REST API through \texttt{ccxt}, which needs no key. A candle that opens at $t_0$ with width $w$ is kept only once $t_0+w\le t_{\text{now}}$, so a candle that is still forming never reaches an agent. The 4-h store used for training is updated incrementally. The console requests candles from the last stored open time, lets incoming rows replace overlapping ones, and writes the merged file to a temporary path before renaming it over the old one. If the request fails, the saved file is left untouched and the tape is marked stale. The 15-min and 1-h tapes keep their own caches of at most twice the display window (1{,}000 candles, or 500 for 4\,h), so a faster timeframe cannot overwrite the training data. Every 20\,s the console polls for newly closed candles and for the last traded price. The price is only displayed, and every decision uses closed candles.

\emph{Labels.} When a tape is opened, one HMM is fitted on its first $\min(80,\max(20,\lfloor n/5\rfloor))$ rows, its states are named by the moment rule, and the rest of the window is decoded in one pass. Each candle that closes later is decoded by a model fitted on all rows before it, with the last issued label carried across. This is faster than the full tracker but not identical to it. Section~\ref{sec:native} scores the live window with the tracker.

\emph{Paper books.} Both agents start flat with unit wealth on the same tape. At each closed candle $t$ each agent chooses $a_t$ from its own observation. When candle $t{+}1$ closes, its wealth is multiplied by $e^{R_t}$, with $R_t$ from~\eqref{eq:reward} including the 10-bp turnover cost. The desk plots both wealth curves, each agent's latest fills and the regime vector fed to the conditioned agent. Sliders let the user override the four regime probabilities for the open candle (rescaled to sum to one), and optionally its features and held position. This applies $\mathrm{do}(p{=}p')$ to the conditioned agent alone and shows its bet next to the blind twin's, so the verifier's interventions can be explored by hand.

\subsection{Implementation details}
Table~\ref{tab:hyper} lists the settings. PPO uses the Stable-Baselines3 defaults for clipping (0.2) and epochs per update (10). Each agent trains for $\max(8192, 10\,N_{\text{train}})$ steps, which is ten passes over its training window: 10{,}800, 39{,}370, 67{,}940 and 96{,}510 steps for the four folds. HMMs are fitted with \texttt{hmmlearn}. If EM leaves a state with an all-zero transition row, that row is reset to uniform before decoding. The full pipeline (292 tracker refits, eight PPO trainings and the audit) ran in 6.6 minutes on a laptop CPU. All 32 unit tests of the repository pass. They include tests for feature leakage, the closed-form distance and label stability under a component swap. Others check that later bars never change a closed window and that a failed Binance request leaves the saved candles untouched. Further tests check that a refresh keeps older rows and replaces overlapping ones, and that the faster timeframes never write to the training cache. The last three cover the native-twin recipe: a windowed episode is one contiguous stretch of the series, no window replays the whole series, and the reward scale multiplies return and cost alike. Table~\ref{tab:hyper} is the four-hour study. The native recipe in Section~\ref{sec:tfproto} uses an entropy coefficient of 0.01.

\subsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}
\emph{Blind twin.} The same PPO agent without $p_t$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:twins}).
\emph{Buy-and-hold.} A constant position $a_t=1$.
\emph{Archived moment-sort run.} The repository keeps the outputs of the pipeline as it stood before the tracker was introduced. That version fitted one HMM per fold and named states with $\ell$ alone, and everything else was identical. Its blind-twin and buy-and-hold results match ours exactly, so the labels are the only change.
\emph{Moment sorting on the same refits.} To isolate the identity rule, we also re-name every one of the 292 refits with $\ell$ instead of~\eqref{eq:match}. This variant is used for label statistics only.
\emph{Reference policies for the verifier.} The rule $a=\mathrm{clip}(p^\top w)$ with $w=(1,-0.4,0.5,-1)$ over (bull, bear, calm, crisis) is faithful by construction. A constant $a=0.5$ ignores the regime input, and $a=\mathrm{clip}(-p^\top w)$ inverts it.

\subsection{Metrics}
Strategy log returns follow~\eqref{eq:reward} with the realised positions. We report the annualised Sharpe ratio $\sqrt{2190}\,\bar R/\hat s_R$ and the Sortino ratio $\sqrt{2190}\,\bar R/\sqrt{\overline{\min(R,0)^2}}$, using 2{,}190 four-hour bars per year. Each comes with a 95\% bootstrap interval. We also report maximum drawdown and total return. Every metric is additionally computed within each dwell-smoothed regime. For the tracker we report label shares, switches per year, run lengths, the mean matched $\Wtwo^2$ cost, and an identity-jump statistic: the $\Wtwo^2$ distance between the probability-weighted components that carry the same name at consecutive refits.

\subsection{Timeframe-native twins}
\label{sec:tfproto}
\emph{Data.} The 15-min and 1-h tapes get twins of their own, trained on each timeframe's own candles. We add ETH/USDT as a second asset and treat it exactly like BTC/USDT, with its own tracker pass and its own twins. Binance candles from 1~Jan 2021 to the live snapshot give, after feature warm-up, 202{,}596 bars on 15\,min and 50{,}627 bars on 1\,h for each asset, about 16 and 4 times as many as the 4-h series.

\emph{Tracker.} The tracker keeps the calendar spans of the 4-h study, a six-week burn-in, a weekly refit and a five-day dwell. In bars this is $(B,S,L)=(1{,}008,168,120)$ on 1\,h and $(4{,}032,672,480)$ on 15\,min. It runs once over each full series, so the live labels continue those of the training data.

\emph{Training recipe.} One episode over the full 1-h training window, with the unscaled four-hour reward and no entropy bonus, sent both BTC twins towards a constant long position. The native twins are therefore trained on random windows. Each episode is a randomly placed stretch of 2{,}000 consecutive training bars, and the agent starts it flat. Training multiplies the reward~\eqref{eq:reward} by 100 and discards that scale at evaluation. Each twin trains for 500{,}000 steps.

\emph{Frozen file.} The validation grid in Section~\ref{sec:native} changes one factor at a time and scores only the validation split. No cell met the pre-registered bar, and the baseline cell had the lowest mean deficit (about 0.985). \texttt{configs/final.yaml} is the native recipe: raw reward, scale 100, entropy coefficient 0.01, uniform 2{,}000-bar windows, tracker v1, no regime perturbation and no faithfulness regularizer. The agents are not regularized. Tracker v2 was one cell of the grid and was not selected.

\emph{Splits.} For validation, twins trained on bars before 22~Sep 2024 16:00~UTC are scored on the following year. For the holdout, twins retrained on bars before 22~Sep 2025 16:00~UTC are scored on the year up to the live window, and the same frozen weights are scored on the live window. Holdout and live were scored once and were not used to choose the recipe. The reported native runs use seeds 42 through 46.

\emph{Regime grouping.} On fast candles the five-day dwell outlasts almost every run of the Viterbi path, so the hard label collapses to a single name over nearly the whole series: calm on BTC 1\,h and ETH 15\,min, bear on BTC 15\,min, and bear on 94.7\% of ETH 1-h bars. Per-regime results for the native twins are therefore grouped by the most likely regime $\argmax_g p_t(g)$, the input the agent actually sees. Regime names are fixed per tape at burn-in, so the same name need not mean the same market state on different tapes.

\subsection{Live evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:liveproto}
\emph{Window.} The last price used by the walk-forward study is the close of the 22~Sep 2026 12:00~UTC candle. The live window scores every candle that opened from 16:00~UTC (21:30~IST) that day until a snapshot taken on 13~Oct 2026 at 10:47~UTC. The last candle of each tape is dropped because its next return is not yet known. This leaves 1{,}994 decisions on 15-min candles and 497 on 1-h candles, over the same 21 calendar days. The candles are frozen with the study script, so the numbers below can be reproduced offline.

\emph{Agents.} The native twins of Sec.~\ref{sec:tfproto} are trained on bars before 22~Sep 2025, for BTC and for ETH, at seeds 42 through 46. Weights stay fixed. Both twins start flat at the first live candle and carry their positions forward. Buy-and-hold enters at the same candle. The four-hour results in this paper are the out-of-fold study of Section~\ref{sec:identity}.

\emph{Labels.} Labels come from the causal tracker of Sec.~\ref{sec:method}, on each timeframe's own candles from 2021, with the calendar-scaled spans of Sec.~\ref{sec:tfproto}. The console fits a faster labelling when a tape is opened. Every live number in Section~\ref{sec:native} uses the tracker.

\emph{Why native twins.} The same features are much smaller on a faster tape: the median $\sigma_{20}$ is 0.14\% on 15\,min and 0.31\% on 1\,h, against 0.61\% on 4\,h and 1.04\% over the four-hour training data. A four-hour conditioned agent transferred onto those tapes turned over 0.115 and 0.104 units per bar. The native twins are trained on the tape they trade.

\emph{Statistics.} For the native twins we report the mean and the standard deviation across the five seeds. Sharpe ratios use 35{,}040 and 8{,}760 bars per year at 15\,min and 1\,h. ``Beats'' is the share of seeds whose conditioned log-return sum exceeds the blind twin. Ablation is the legacy pass rate. The four-hour Sharpe intervals in Tables~\ref{tab:folds} and~\ref{tab:perf} use the i.i.d.\ bootstrap of Table~\ref{tab:hyper} and were not recomputed here.

\subsection{Regime identity under refits}
\label{sec:identity}
Fig.~\ref{fig:regimes} shows the price with the tracker's labels, the bear and crisis probabilities, and both labellings. Table~\ref{tab:labels} compares them. Moment sorting assigns a different name than the $\Wtwo$ rule to at least one component in 291 of the 292 refits, and to 49.6\% of all components. Its labels are semantically unstable. Over the sample, the bars it calls \emph{bull} have a negative mean return ($-0.8$\,bp), and its \emph{crisis} bars are only slightly more volatile than its \emph{calm} bars (1.14\% against 1.04\%). The $\Wtwo$ tracker keeps the ordering the names promise: crisis bars average 2.18\% volatility and $-8.1$\,bp per bar, against 0.97\% and $+1.5$\,bp for calm. It also switches less often (5.5 against 7.5 times per year). Its matched cost is 4.9 times lower ($1.88$ against $9.13\times10^{-4}$), and the identity jump of the crisis name falls by a factor of 7.4.

These gains have a price. Matching~\eqref{eq:match} is non-injective in 291 of the 292 refits. Fig.~\ref{fig:templates} shows why. The bull template inherits the burn-in's high-volatility, high-return component from early 2021. Later fits never produce a component near it, so it receives members in only 35 refits. \subsection{Faithfulness}
Fig.~\ref{fig:faith} reports the pass rate of each check. The reference policies behave as designed. The faithful rule passes on 97.9\% of bars; it fails ablation only where its output is already within 0.15 of zero. The ignoring and inverted rules pass on no bar.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/faithfulness.pdf}
\caption{Share of out-of-fold bars passing each check, for the PPO agent trained on $\Wtwo$ labels, the archived PPO agent trained on moment-sorted labels, and three reference policies scored on the $\Wtwo$ probabilities. Numbers give the share passing all three checks.}

For the conditioned PPO agent with $\Wtwo$ labels, ablation passes on 60.7\% of bars, with a median $\lvert\Delta^{\text{ab}}\rvert$ of 0.42, so the regime input is clearly load-bearing. The counterfactual check, however, passes on only 0.9\% of bars, and the combined faithfulness rate is 0.1\% (11 of 11{,}430 bars). The archived agent reached 33.5\%. Fig.~\ref{fig:interv} explains the failure. Forcing \emph{bear} sends the median position to $-1$, and the forced-bear position lies below the forced-calm position on 76.5\% of bars. The agent has learned a defensive response, but it is keyed to bear. Forcing \emph{crisis} gives a median of $+0.64$, which is defensive relative to calm on only 30.1\% of bars. Forcing \emph{bull} gives $+0.53$, below the realised median of $+0.74$. Taken separately, the two directions of the counterfactual check pass on 23.3\% (crisis cuts the position) and 19.9\% (bull raises it) of eligible bars, and their conjunction almost never holds. The crisis sweep in Fig.~\ref{fig:interv}(a) tells the same story. For bear and crisis bars the mean position \emph{rises} as crisis probability is injected.

This behaviour follows from the regime layer. The tracker's probabilities are almost one-hot (mean maximum 0.975), and crisis occupies only 3.8\% of bars. One-hot bull never occurs in training after burn-in. The counterfactual inputs $e_{\text{bull}}$ and $e_{\text{crisis}}$ therefore lie far from, or entirely outside, the data the policy was fitted on. What the agent does there is an extrapolation, so the check fails without the agent being ``miswired'' on the inputs it actually meets. The archived labels kept all four regimes populated (bull 6.1\%, crisis 12.4\% of test bars), and their counterfactuals were in distribution. That, more than any quality of the agent, explains the archived 33.5\%. Two further caveats concern the checks themselves. The counterfactual rank uses $\argmax_g p_t(g)$, which agrees with the dwell-smoothed label on only 78.8\% of bars. And the sensitivity check is weak, since a flat curve passes it. On 18~Jun~2022, near the crash low, $p_t(\text{crisis})=1$ and the agent is fully long. Every intervention returns $+1$, so ablation and counterfactual fail while sensitivity passes. The ignoring policy passes sensitivity on every bar for the same reason.

The lesson is methodological. Swapping one well-motivated regime component for another, with architecture, data and training unchanged, improved label semantics and reversed the faithfulness verdict. A verdict cannot be carried over to a modified pipeline.

\subsection{Five-seed native twins}
\label{sec:native}
The native recipe is the control cell of a one-factor grid. Each cell trains both twins for 500{,}000 steps on the validation split only (window 2{,}000, reward scale 100, two threads, seed 42), on BTC and ETH at 1\,h and 15\,min. The factors, one at a time, are the reward (raw, excess over buy-and-hold, differential Sharpe), the window sampler (uniform, regime-balanced, return-balanced), the entropy coefficient (0.01 or 0.02), a per-episode perturbation of the regime probabilities (0, 0.05 or 0.1), and the tracker (v1 or v2). Returns are not a selection input. A tape would pass only if both twins had position standard deviation above 0.05, both spent less than 80\% of bars at $|a|>0.95$, and ablation moved the conditioned agent on at least 10\% of bars. The score of a cell is the mean, over the four tapes, of the summed relative shortfalls from those three bars.

No cell passed. Every validation twin still spent more than 90\% of bars at $|a|>0.95$. The baseline had the lowest mean deficit, about 0.985. The next cell, perturbation probability 0.1, was 1.33, and excess-over-buy-and-hold was the worst at 2.65: on BTC 1\,h both of those twins finished with position standard deviation 0. Differential Sharpe did raise the standard deviation, often above 0.9, but the mass stayed on the rails and the BTC 15-min blind twin was constant. Return-balanced sampling produced an ablation rate of 73\% on BTC 1\,h, with standard deviations 0.56 and 0.84 and extreme shares of 0.98 and 1.00. No factor reduced the baseline deficit, so the pre-registered stack of helpful factors was empty and no combination was trained. \texttt{configs/final.yaml} stayed the native recipe: raw reward, scale 100, entropy coefficient 0.01, uniform 2{,}000-bar windows, tracker v1, no perturbation and no faithfulness regularizer. The agents below are not regularized.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
%======================================================================
\emph{Within-window smoothing.} Each HMM is fitted only on rows before its window, but decoding uses Viterbi and forward--backward over the whole 42-bar window. The probability at a bar therefore conditions on up to 41 later bars of the same window, and the dwell merge similarly needs a run to finish before it can be judged. Labels are never revised after their window closes, but they are not strictly filtered. A forward-only (filtered) posterior and an online dwell rule would remove this leakage. Until then, the per-regime results should be read with this caveat. The native tapes use the same decoder inside each weekly refit window.

\emph{Template starvation.} Non-injective matching with a frozen unmatched template removes the bull regime on the four-hour tracker (Table~\ref{tab:labels}). One-to-one matching is not a complete fix either (Sec.~\ref{sec:identity}). Template birth and death, and a longer or multi-cycle burn-in, are natural next steps. Tracker v2 was scored on the native validation split and was not selected. On the five-seed four-hour repetition it loses on every seed (Section~\ref{sec:fourhseeds}).

\emph{Counterfactual design.} One-hot interventions test the four-hour policy off its training distribution, because template starvation leaves some of those inputs unseen. Verifier v2 draws counterfactual regime vectors from the training distribution and rejects a flat sensitivity curve. On the five-seed twins that rate is at or below the legacy rate, and both are small (Fig.~\ref{fig:v2faith}). The agents were not trained with a faithfulness penalty.

\emph{Statistics.} The i.i.d.\ bootstrap ignores the serial dependence of four-hour returns. A stationary block bootstrap would give wider intervals for Tables~\ref{tab:folds} and~\ref{tab:perf}. Tables~\ref{tab:folds}--\ref{tab:regime} remain the published single run. Section~\ref{sec:fourhseeds} repeats that recipe for five seeds and does not replace those tables. The repetition uses a circular block bootstrap with block length $\lceil\sqrt{n}\rceil$ and 1{,}000 resamples. The native comparison uses five seeds on BTC/USDT and ETH/USDT. It reports the standard deviation of the five numbers and the share of seeds on which the conditioned log-return sum is larger.

\emph{Live evidence.} The live window is 21 days, from 22~Sep to 13~Oct 2026, and buy-and-hold lost money on every native tape. The window can show the direction of a gap. It cannot establish one. Paper fills happen at the candle close with a flat 10-bp cost, ignoring slippage, latency and the funding cost of short positions. Across the five seeds the mean share of bars with $|a|>0.95$ is 97--100\% for every twin. The validation grid did not meet the pre-registered bar, and holdout and live were not used to choose the recipe. The console's start-up labelling is faster than the tracker, but it decodes its whole window at once, which smooths within the window over hundreds of candles. Its on-screen wealth curves are illustrative, and every live number reported here comes from the frozen study and the tracker.

\emph{Scope.} The thresholds $\delta_{\text{ab}}$ and $\delta_{\text{cf}}$ and the defensive ordering $\rho$ are design choices. Transaction costs are a flat 10\,bp, and the agents were built directly on Stable-Baselines3. Ndoutoumou \emph{et al.} place the regime signal inside the FinRL stack~\cite{ndoutoumou2025hmm}. The four-hour sections use tracker v1 and the one-hot verifier. The native agents are not regularized.

\section{Conclusion}
%======================================================================
We built a regime-conditioned RL trading agent with a Wasserstein identity tracker and a suite that intervenes on the regime input of every decision. On BTC/USDT four-hour data the tracker kept a stable volatility ordering across 292 causal refits and starved the bull template. The conditioned agent used those labels: removing them changed its position on 60.7\% of the 11{,}430 out-of-fold bars, and it reduced exposure in bear and crisis segments. Only 0.1\% of bars passed all three checks, against 33.5\% for an archived moment-sorted labelling. The out-of-fold Sharpe ratios are $-0.16$ for the conditioned agent, $-1.12$ for the blind twin and $0.35$ for buy-and-hold. These four-hour numbers were not recomputed here.

The native-twin result is five seeds of the frozen recipe on BTC/USDT and ETH/USDT, at 15\,min and 1\,h. The validation grid did not meet the pre-registered bar, so \texttt{configs/final.yaml} stayed the native recipe: raw reward, scale 100, entropy coefficient 0.01, uniform 2{,}000-bar windows, tracker v1, no perturbation and no faithfulness regularizer. The baseline had the lowest mean deficit, about 0.985. Holdout and live were scored once and were not used to choose the recipe. Every twin spends 97--100\% of bars at $|a|>0.95$ on average across the five seeds. The ETH 1\,h blind twin and the BTC 15-min live blind twin are flat, and the mean holdout Sharpe ratio is negative for both twins. The conditioned twin beats the blind twin on a majority of seeds in two of eight windows only, BTC 1\,h holdout ($+2.8\%$ against $-26.1\%$, beats share 0.80) and ETH 1\,h live ($+0.4\%$ against $-5.5\%$, beats share 0.80). Table~\ref{tab:v2seeds} lists the other six windows, including BTC 15\,min live, where the blind position standard deviation is 0 on all five seeds, and ETH 1\,h holdout, where the conditioned mean is one large path (beats share 0.20). The conditioned agent does not win overall. The agents are not regularized, and both faithfulness rates are small.

The four-hour recipe was then repeated on seeds 42--46. Tracker v1's conditioned mean return of $+57.8\%$ is seed 46 at $+515\%$, from one test fold at $+870\%$. The other four seeds lose money, and the mean Sharpe ratio is $-0.27$. Tracker v2 and moment-sorted names lose on average ($-83.6\%$ and $-44.0\%$). Faithfulness stays under 3\% for both trackers and is 12.5\% for moment-sorted names. On the native validation twins the raw Gaussian mean is outside $[-1,1]$ on 95.6--99.8\% of bars, so the clip is what pins the executed position to the rail. Neither a tanh execution nor a five-point discrete action passed the pre-registered bar on all four tapes, and holdout and live were not scored for those heads. A faithfulness verdict has to be recomputed when the regime layer or the training recipe changes.
