\begin{abstract}
Regime-aware reinforcement-learning (RL) trading agents are usually judged by back-tested returns, which cannot show whether the regime signal drives their decisions. We pair a Wasserstein regime-identity tracker for walk-forward hidden-Markov-model refits with three interventions on the regime input, and compare a proximal policy optimisation (PPO) agent with a twin that lacks that input. On BTC/USDT four-hour bars the tracker keeps a stable volatility ordering across 292 causal refits and lowers the mean template-matching cost by a factor of 4.9, but it starves the bull template and issues no bull label after burn-in. Ablation moves the conditioned position on 60.7\% of 11{,}430 out-of-fold bars, yet only 0.1\% of bars pass all three checks (0.25\% of the 4{,}390 bars on which the position limit allows the counterfactual move), against 33.5\% for moment-sorted labels. Repeating that study on five seeds, the conditioned mean return of $+57.8\%$ is one seed at $+515\%$; the other four lose and the mean Sharpe ratio is $-0.27$. Five seeds of timeframe-native twins on BTC/USDT and ETH/USDT at 15\,min and 1\,h, with the recipe frozen on validation, give a negative mean holdout Sharpe ratio for both twins, and the conditioned twin beats the blind twin on a majority of seeds in two of eight windows only. Those twins hold $|a|>0.95$ on 97--100\% of bars. Validation screens of a tanh head, a five-level discrete head and a mean-variance reward, alone and combined, found no setting that moves the policy off the rails while keeping it responsive.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%======================================================================
Cryptocurrency markets move through very different conditions within a few years. Bitcoin rose sharply in 2021, lost roughly three quarters of its value between late 2021 and late 2022, and then spent 2023--2026 alternating between recoveries and drawdowns. A natural remedy for such non-stationarity is to detect the prevailing regime and hand it to the policy as an extra input. Hidden-Markov-model (HMM) regimes have been fed to RL portfolio agents with stronger reported risk-adjusted returns~\cite{ndoutoumou2025hmm}, and regimes have been used for sector portfolios~\cite{zhang2025regimefolio} and asset allocation~\cite{zhang2020regimealloc}.

What this work rarely checks is whether the regime input matters. An agent can report that it ``cut exposure because a crisis was detected'' while its computation ignores the crisis probability or responds to it in the wrong direction. An explanation that is easy to understand is not necessarily faithful to the computation behind it~\cite{mamalakis2025enigma}. A second problem sits inside the detector. An HMM refitted on new data returns its states in arbitrary order. Sorting states by their moments after every fit forces four names onto whatever four components the latest fit produces. We instead keep persistent bull, bear, calm and crisis templates and match each new component to the nearest template by the closed-form 2-Wasserstein distance, so a name survives a refit only when the component still resembles it.

We ask whether Wasserstein tracking gives labels that keep their meaning across walk-forward refits, and whether an RL agent conditioned on them depends on them in the way their names imply. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A walk-forward Wasserstein template tracker for a four-state Gaussian HMM, with matching, aggregation and template smoothing written as equations that match the released code.
  \item A per-decision verification suite of three interventions on the regime input, validated on reference policies that are faithful, regime-ignoring and inverted by construction, and a twin experiment in which conditioned and blind PPO agents differ only by that input.
  \item A four-hour BTC/USDT study in which the regime input is load-bearing but the faithfulness rate collapses from 33.5\% to 0.1\%, traced to template starvation and out-of-distribution counterfactuals, with a five-seed repetition showing that its mean return is one seed.
  \item A five-seed study of timeframe-native twins on BTC/USDT and ETH/USDT at 15\,min and 1\,h with a recipe frozen on validation, and a negative result on the position rails: neither other action heads nor a mean-variance reward produced a policy that uses the interior of $[-1,1]$ while staying responsive.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
%======================================================================
\emph{Regimes and RL for portfolios.} Ndoutoumou \emph{et al.} pass a Gaussian-HMM regime into RL agents on a Dow Jones portfolio and report better Sharpe and Sortino ratios than the same agents without it~\cite{ndoutoumou2025hmm}; the comparison is on back-tested returns and does not test whether the policy uses the regime. Regimes have also been detected with directional-change indicators in foreign exchange~\cite{tsang2018regime}, classified with explainable models for asset allocation~\cite{zhang2020regimealloc}, and used for sectoral portfolios in non-stationary markets~\cite{zhang2025regimefolio}. Deep RL for portfolio choice is surveyed in~\cite{mortaji2024review,rout2026review} and applied to asset-weight management~\cite{wang2022drl}, dynamic capital allocation~\cite{tamuly2024drl}, real-time rebalancing with PPO and DDPG~\cite{naik2025realtime}, single-stock portfolios~\cite{huang2025singlestock}, A-share portfolios under trading constraints~\cite{gao2025drl} and adversarial market conditions~\cite{shrirao2025adversarial}. Across this work a higher Sharpe ratio is treated as evidence that the agent has adapted. None of it checks whether a named regime input is what the policy uses.

\emph{Explainability and causality.} Mamalakis \emph{et al.} separate a faithful explanation from a merely comprehensible one~\cite{mamalakis2025enigma}. Wiratsin and Ragkhitwetsagul note the lack of standard evaluation metrics for explainable AI~\cite{wiratsin2025xai}, Yeo \emph{et al.} survey post-hoc explanations in finance~\cite{yeo2025financial}, and Reinhardt and Buchholz argue that explainability in high-stakes domains is an obligation~\cite{reinhardt2025xai}. Causal models of the market have been built for stock-trend forecasting~\cite{qiu2025cistf} and for decisions in changing environments~\cite{katamaneni2025causal}. Our tests differ in kind: the policy is a known function, so setting one named input measures that input's effect on the action, not a causal effect in the market.

\section{Preliminaries}
%======================================================================
Let $x_t\in\R^3$ be the regime features at bar $t$. A $K$-state Gaussian HMM has latent states $z_t$, a transition matrix $A$ and emissions $x_t\mid z_t=k\sim\mathcal N(m_k,\mathrm{diag}(v_k))$, fitted by expectation--maximisation (EM). The posterior $\gamma_t(k)=P(z_t=k\mid x_{1:n})$ comes from the forward--backward recursion and the most likely path from Viterbi decoding. The likelihood is invariant to permuting state indices, so two fits on overlapping data can return the same regimes under different indices.

For Gaussians the 2-Wasserstein distance has a closed form. With diagonal covariances $\Sigma_i=\mathrm{diag}(s_i^2)$ the covariance term reduces to $\lVert s_1-s_2\rVert^2$, so
\begin{equation}
\Wtwo^2\bigl(\mathcal N(m_1,\Sigma_1),\mathcal N(m_2,\Sigma_2)\bigr)=\lVert m_1-m_2\rVert^2+\lVert s_1-s_2\rVert^2 .
\label{eq:w2diag}
\end{equation}
It compares location and spread on the feature scale at $O(d)$ cost.

A trained deterministic policy $a=\pi_\theta(o)$ is a known function of its observation $o=(u,p)$, where $p$ is the regime-probability block. We write $\pi_\theta(u,\mathrm{do}(p{=}p'))$ for the action when $p$ is set to $p'$ and everything else is held fixed. Because $\pi_\theta$ is available in closed form, this needs no causal identification assumptions. A regime signal is \emph{load-bearing} if such interventions change the action, and \emph{faithful to its names} if the change has the sign the names imply.

\subsection{Causal features and walk-forward refits}
Let $C_t$ be the close of bar $t$. The features are the log return $r_t=\log(C_t/C_{t-1})$, the 20-bar rolling standard deviation $\sigma_t$ of $r$, the trend $\tau_t=C_t/\mathrm{SMA}_{20}(C)_t-1$, the 14-bar Wilder RSI scaled to $[0,1]$, and the MACD histogram divided by $C_t$. Each feature at $t$ uses rows $\le t$ only, and a unit test checks that features on a truncated history equal the prefix of the full computation. The regime model uses $x_t=(r_t,\sigma_t,\tau_t)$. The agent sees all five as $f_t$, rescaled to unit order by a fixed map $\phi$.

The tracker makes one forward pass over the series (Fig.~\ref{fig:timeline}). The first $B=252$ bars (six weeks) are a burn-in. Refit $j$ starts at $t_j=B+jS$ with stride $S=42$ bars (one week). A four-state diagonal HMM is fitted by EM (200 iterations, seed 42) to the rows strictly before $t_j$, standardised with that window's mean $\bar x$ and standard deviation $s$, and decodes the window $W_j=\{t_j,\dots,t_j+S-1\}$. Component parameters are mapped back to feature units, $\mu_k=\hat m_k\odot s+\bar x$ and $\sigma_k=\sqrt{\hat v_k}\odot s$. The burn-in fit is the only place where names come from moments: of the two components with the lowest mean return, the less volatile is \emph{bear} and the other \emph{crisis}; of the two highest, the less volatile is \emph{calm} and the other \emph{bull}. This yields templates $(\bar\mu_g,\bar\sigma_g)$ for $g\in\G=\{\text{bull},\text{bear},\text{calm},\text{crisis}\}$.

\subsection{Wasserstein identity matching and template update}
At refit $j$ the cost of naming component $k$ as regime $g$ is the diagonal distance~\eqref{eq:w2diag}, $D_{kg}=\lVert\mu_k-\bar\mu_g\rVert^2+\lVert\sigma_k-\bar\sigma_g\rVert^2$, and every component takes its nearest template,
\begin{equation}
\pi_j(k)=\argmin_{g\in\G} D_{kg}.
\label{eq:match}
\end{equation}
The matching need not be one-to-one. The regime probabilities for $t\in W_j$ are the summed posteriors
\begin{equation}
p_t(g)=\sum_{k:\,\pi_j(k)=g}\gamma_t(k),
\label{eq:agg}
\end{equation}
and an unmatched template has $p_t(g)=0$. After $W_j$ is labelled, every matched template moves a step $\eta=0.2$ toward its members. With $w_k=\sum_{t\in W_j}\gamma_t(k)$, member set $M_g=\{k:\pi_j(k)=g\}$ and $\omega_k=w_k/\sum_{k'\in M_g}w_{k'}$,
\begin{equation}
\bar\mu_g\leftarrow(1-\eta)\,\bar\mu_g+\eta\sum_{k\in M_g}\omega_k\mu_k ,
\label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
and likewise for $\bar\sigma_g$. A template with $M_g=\emptyset$ is left unchanged. The hard label $\hat h_t=\pi_j(\hat z_t)$ names the Viterbi state; runs shorter than $L=30$ bars (five days) are merged into the preceding label. Hard labels are used only for reporting. The agent sees $p_t$.

\subsection{Twin agents}
\label{sec:twins}
At bar $t$ the agent chooses a target position $a_t\in[-1,1]$ and receives
\begin{equation}
R_t=a_t\,r_{t+1}-c\,\lvert a_t-a_{t-1}\rvert,\qquad c=10^{-3},
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
the next log return less a 10-basis-point turnover cost. The conditioned agent observes $o_t=[\phi(f_t),a_{t-1},p_t]\in\R^{10}$ and the blind twin $[\phi(f_t),a_{t-1}]\in\R^{6}$. Both are Stable-Baselines3 PPO agents with identical settings; the only architectural difference is four extra input weights.

\subsection{Verification suite}
For each out-of-fold bar we freeze the policy, the features and the previous position, and intervene on $p$ alone. Let $a_t=\pi_\theta(u_t,p_t)$, $e_g$ the one-hot vector of regime $g$, and $\rho$ a defensive rank with $\rho(\text{bull})=0<\rho(\text{calm})=1<\rho(\text{bear})=2<\rho(\text{crisis})=3$.

\emph{Ablation.} The regime input is load-bearing at $t$ if
\begin{equation}
\Delta^{\text{ab}}_t=\bigl\lvert a_t-\pi_\theta(u_t,\mathrm{do}(p{=}\mathbf 0))\bigr\rvert\ \ge\ \delta_{\text{ab}}=0.15 .
\end{equation}

\emph{Counterfactual.} With $g_t=\argmax_g p_t(g)$, forcing crisis must cut the position and forcing bull must raise it, each by at least $\delta_{\text{cf}}=0.10$:
\begin{align}
\rho(g_t)<3 &\Rightarrow a_t-\pi_\theta(u_t,\mathrm{do}(p{=}e_{\text{crisis}}))\ge\delta_{\text{cf}},\\
\rho(g_t)>0 &\Rightarrow \pi_\theta(u_t,\mathrm{do}(p{=}e_{\text{bull}}))-a_t\ge\delta_{\text{cf}}.
\end{align}

\emph{Sensitivity.} For $q\in\{0,0.1,\dots,1\}$ we inject a crisis probability $q$ and rescale the rest,
\begin{equation}
p^{(q)}=(1-q)\,\frac{p_t^{\setminus c}}{\lVert p_t^{\setminus c}\rVert_1}+q\,e_{\text{crisis}},
\end{equation}
where $p_t^{\setminus c}$ is $p_t$ with its crisis entry set to zero. The curve $y_i=\pi_\theta(u_t,\mathrm{do}(p{=}p^{(q_i)}))$ passes if it rises at most once.

A bar is \emph{faithful} when all three checks pass, and the faithfulness rate $F$ is the share of faithful bars. \emph{Verifier v2} replaces the one-hot vectors by the nearest training-set probability vectors whose argmax is crisis or bull, rejects a flat sensitivity curve, and marks a bar untestable rather than failed when the training set has no such vector.

\section{Experimental Setup}
%======================================================================
\subsection{Four-hour study}
We use Binance BTC/USDT four-hour candles from 1~Jan 2021 to 22~Sep 2026 (12{,}544 bars; 12{,}510 after feature warm-up). Four expanding walk-forward folds start with 1{,}080 training bars, and each test block of about 2{,}857 bars is scored by agents trained only on the bars before it (Table~\ref{tab:folds}). The test blocks give 11{,}430 out-of-fold bars from 5~Jul 2021 to 22~Sep 2026. Labels come from the single causal tracker pass.

\emph{Settings.} HMMs use \texttt{hmmlearn} with minimum covariance $10^{-3}$. The policy is an MLP $64{-}64$ with $\tanh$ units and a Gaussian action head clipped to $[-1,1]$. PPO uses learning rate $3{\times}10^{-4}$, $\gamma{=}0.99$, $\lambda_{\text{GAE}}{=}0.95$, entropy coefficient 0, $n_{\text{steps}}{=}256$, batch 64, clip 0.2, 10 epochs, seed 42, and $\max(8192,10\,N_{\text{train}})$ steps per agent (ten passes over the training window). The full pipeline (292 refits, eight PPO trainings and the audit) ran in 6.6 minutes on a laptop CPU, and all 32 unit tests pass, including feature leakage, the closed-form distance and label stability under a component swap.

\emph{Baselines.}
\label{sec:baselines}
The blind twin (Sec.~\ref{sec:twins}) and buy-and-hold ($a_t=1$). The \emph{archived moment-sort run} is the pipeline before the tracker was introduced: one HMM per fold named by moments alone, everything else identical. Its blind-twin and buy-and-hold results match ours exactly, so the labels are the only change. For label statistics we also re-name all 292 refits by moment sorting instead of~\eqref{eq:match}. The verifier is validated on three reference policies over (bull, bear, calm, crisis): $a=\mathrm{clip}(p^\top w)$ with $w=(1,-0.4,0.5,-1)$ is faithful by construction, $a=0.5$ ignores the regime, and $a=\mathrm{clip}(-p^\top w)$ inverts it.

\emph{Metrics.} We report the annualised Sharpe ratio $\sqrt{2190}\,\bar R/\hat s_R$ and the Sortino ratio with 95\% percentile intervals from 1{,}000 i.i.d.\ bootstrap resamples, maximum drawdown and total return, each also within each dwell-smoothed regime. For the tracker we report label shares, switches per year, run lengths and the mean matched $\Wtwo^2$ cost.

\subsection{Timeframe-native twins}
\label{sec:tfproto}
The 15-min and 1-h tapes of BTC/USDT and ETH/USDT get twins trained on their own candles: 202{,}596 bars on 15\,min and 50{,}627 on 1\,h per asset after warm-up. The tracker keeps the calendar spans of the four-hour study, $(B,S,L)=(1{,}008,168,120)$ on 1\,h and $(4{,}032,672,480)$ on 15\,min, in one pass per series. A single episode over the full training window sent both BTC 1-h twins towards a constant long position, so each episode is a randomly placed stretch of 2{,}000 training bars started flat, the reward~\eqref{eq:reward} is multiplied by 100 during training only, and each twin trains for 500{,}000 steps.

\emph{Splits.} For validation, twins trained on bars before 22~Sep 2024 16:00~UTC are scored on the following year. For the holdout, twins retrained on bars before 22~Sep 2025 16:00~UTC are scored on the year up to the live window. The live window scores every candle from 22~Sep 2026 16:00~UTC to a snapshot on 13~Oct 2026 at 10:47~UTC (1{,}994 decisions on 15\,min and 497 on 1\,h, 21 days) with the same frozen weights. Native runs use seeds 42--46. We report the mean and the standard deviation across seeds; Sharpe ratios use 35{,}040 and 8{,}760 bars per year. ``Beats'' is the share of seeds whose conditioned log-return sum exceeds the blind twin's.

\emph{Live console.} A Streamlit console runs both twins on paper money as Binance candles close and never sends an order. Only closed candles reach an agent, a failed request leaves the stored candles untouched, and sliders apply $\mathrm{do}(p{=}p')$ to the conditioned agent so the interventions can be explored by hand. Its start-up labelling is faster than the tracker; every live number reported here comes from the frozen study and the tracker.

\subsection{Faithfulness}
\label{sec:faith}
The reference policies behave as designed (Fig.~\ref{fig:faith}): the faithful rule passes on 97.9\% of bars, failing ablation only where its output is already within 0.15 of zero, and the ignoring and inverted rules pass on no bar. For the conditioned PPO agent, ablation passes on 60.7\% of bars with a median $\lvert\Delta^{\text{ab}}\rvert$ of 0.42, so the regime input is load-bearing. The counterfactual check passes on only 0.9\%, and the combined rate is 0.1\% (11 of 11{,}430 bars), against 33.5\% for the archived agent.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/faithfulness.pdf}
\caption{Share of out-of-fold bars passing each check, for the PPO agent trained on $\Wtwo$ labels, the archived PPO agent trained on moment-sorted labels, and three reference policies scored on the $\Wtwo$ probabilities. Numbers give the share passing all three checks.}
\label{fig:faith}
\end{figure}

Fig.~\ref{fig:interv} explains the failure. Forcing \emph{bear} sends the median position to $-1$, and the forced-bear position lies below the forced-calm position on 76.5\% of bars: the agent has learned a defensive response keyed to bear. Forcing \emph{crisis} gives a median of $+0.64$, defensive relative to calm on only 30.1\% of bars, and forcing \emph{bull} gives $+0.53$, below the realised median of $+0.74$. The two directions of the counterfactual check pass on 23.3\% (crisis cuts) and 19.9\% (bull raises) of eligible bars, and their conjunction almost never holds. The tracker's probabilities are almost one-hot (mean maximum 0.975), crisis occupies 3.8\% of bars, and one-hot bull never occurs in training after burn-in, so $e_{\text{bull}}$ and $e_{\text{crisis}}$ lie far from, or outside, the data the policy was fitted on. The archived labels kept all four regimes populated (bull 6.1\%, crisis 12.4\% of test bars), and their counterfactuals were in distribution. The counterfactual rank uses $\argmax_g p_t(g)$, which agrees with the dwell label on only 78.8\% of bars, and a flat sensitivity curve passes the legacy check.

\emph{Limit-aware re-score.} A counterfactual can also be impossible to pass: an agent within 0.10 of $+1$ cannot raise its position by 0.10 when bull is forced, and one within 0.10 of $-1$ cannot cut it when crisis is forced. Re-scoring the published run with such bars set aside, 7{,}040 of the 11{,}430 bars (61.6\%) are untestable. On the 4{,}390 testable bars the counterfactual check passes on 2.4\% and all three checks on 0.25\%; the 11 passing bars are the same. This is reported beside the 0.1\% headline, not instead of it: removing the position limit as an explanation leaves the faithfulness rate small.

The lesson is methodological. Swapping one well-motivated regime component for another, with architecture, data and training unchanged, improved label semantics and reversed the faithfulness verdict. A verdict cannot be carried over to a modified pipeline.

\subsection{Five-seed native twins}
\label{sec:native}
The native recipe was chosen by a one-factor grid on the validation split only (seed 42, both assets and timeframes). The factors were the reward (raw, excess over buy-and-hold, differential Sharpe), the window sampler (uniform, regime-balanced, return-balanced), the entropy coefficient (0.01, 0.02), a per-episode perturbation of the regime probabilities (0, 0.05, 0.1) and the tracker (v1, v2). \emph{Screens.} Three validation screens followed, each at seed 42 with the clipped Gaussian under the raw reward as the control and the reward, budget and both twins' head otherwise unchanged. The first replaced the head: \texttt{tanh\_env} executes $a=\tanh(u)$, and \texttt{discrete5} chooses among $\{-1,-0.5,0,0.5,1\}$ with the verifier scoring the expected position. The second and third kept the clipped Gaussian or the five-level head and replaced~\eqref{eq:reward} by a mean-variance reward,
\begin{equation}
R^{\text{mv}}_t=a_t\,r_{t+1}-c\,\lvert a_t-a_{t-1}\rvert-\lambda\,a_t^2\sigma_t^2,\qquad \lambda\in\{2,5,10\},
\label{eq:mv}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_t$ the 20-bar volatility known at $t$. A reward linear in the position is maximised at a rail whenever the expected return is non-zero. Under~\eqref{eq:mv} the expected reward, ignoring turnover, is maximised at $a^*=\mathrm{E}[r_{t+1}]/(2\lambda\sigma_t^2)$, an interior position unless the signal is large relative to risk, so this was the principled test of the rail mechanism. The pass bar was fixed before each screen and is the grid bar of Section~\ref{sec:native}: on all four tapes, both twins with position standard deviation above 0.05, both below 0.80 at $|a|>0.95$, and conditioned ablation on at least 10\% of bars. Returns were not an input. Each screen was validation-only, and holdout and live were not scored for any of these cells.

Under the clipped Gaussian the penalty did not move the policies off the rails: at every $\lambda$, every twin on every tape is at $|a|>0.95$ on at least 88.9\% of bars. Under the five-level head the penalty did remove the rails. Every twin is at $\pm1$ on at most 5.9\% of bars, except the ETH 15-min blind twin at $\lambda=2$ (95.9\%). The policies instead collapsed to a single level, usually 0 or $+0.5$. The number of twins holding one level on more than 90\% of bars is 4, 3 and 6 of 8 at $\lambda=2$, 5 and 10, and at $\lambda=10$ three twins hold position 0 on every bar. The number of twins at or below position standard deviation 0.05 rises from 1 of 8 at $\lambda=2$ to 3 of 8 at $\lambda=5$ and $\lambda=10$. A larger $\lambda$ made the collapse worse, not better.

\emph{Interpretation.} A hypothesis consistent with all three screens is that the policies behave as if the features carry little predictable next-bar signal. The best they then find is a fixed position: at a rail under the linear reward, where any small mean return favours a corner, and at an interior level once risk is penalised, where $a^*$ shrinks towards 0 as $\lambda$ grows. On this reading the rails are a symptom of a weak signal, not its cause, which would explain why changing the head or the reward moved the policy's resting point without making it responsive. A direct test of feature predictability was not run, so this remains a hypothesis.

%======================================================================
\section{Limitations and Future Work}
%======================================================================
\emph{Within-window smoothing.} Each HMM is fitted only on earlier rows, but Viterbi and forward--backward decoding over the 42-bar window let a bar's probability condition on up to 41 later bars of that window. Labels are never revised after their window closes, but they are not strictly filtered; per-regime results carry this caveat, and the native tapes use the same decoder.

\emph{Template starvation and counterfactuals.} Non-injective matching with a frozen unmatched template removes the bull regime, and one-to-one matching is not a complete fix (Sec.~\ref{sec:identity}). Tracker v2 restores a thin bull regime but loses on every seed (Sec.~\ref{sec:fourhseeds}). One-hot interventions test the four-hour policy off its training distribution; verifier v2 draws in-distribution vectors and still reports small rates. No agent was trained with a faithfulness penalty.

\emph{Statistics and live evidence.} The i.i.d.\ bootstrap of Tables~\ref{tab:folds} and~\ref{tab:perf} ignores serial dependence, so a block bootstrap would widen those intervals. The live window is 21 days, in which buy-and-hold lost money on every tape; it can show the direction of a gap but cannot establish one. Paper fills at the candle close with a flat 10-bp cost ignore slippage, latency and funding. The thresholds $\delta_{\text{ab}}$ and $\delta_{\text{cf}}$ and the ordering $\rho$ are design choices.

\emph{Position rails.} No action head or reward tested here produced a policy that uses the interior of $[-1,1]$ while staying responsive: the clipped Gaussian and the tanh head stay on the rails, and the five-level head with or without a mean-variance penalty settles on a fixed level on some tapes. The untested next step is a direct predictability test of the features for the next-bar return, and richer inputs if that test finds little signal.

%======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%======================================================================
We built a regime-conditioned RL trading agent with a Wasserstein identity tracker and a suite that intervenes on the regime input of every decision. On BTC/USDT four-hour data the tracker kept a stable volatility ordering across 292 causal refits and starved the bull template. The conditioned agent used those labels, since removing them changed its position on 60.7\% of the 11{,}430 out-of-fold bars and it reduced exposure in bear and crisis segments, yet only 0.1\% of bars passed all three checks (0.25\% of the bars the position limit leaves testable), against 33.5\% for moment-sorted labels. Its out-of-fold Sharpe ratio was $-0.16$, against $-1.12$ for the blind twin and $0.35$ for buy-and-hold, and a five-seed repetition shows that the conditioned mean return of $+57.8\%$ is one seed at $+515\%$, with a mean Sharpe ratio of $-0.27$.

Five seeds of timeframe-native twins with a recipe frozen on validation give a negative mean holdout Sharpe ratio for both twins, and the conditioned twin beats the blind twin on a majority of seeds in two of eight windows only. Those twins hold $|a|>0.95$ on 97--100\% of bars. Neither a tanh or five-level action head nor a mean-variance reward produced a policy that uses the interior of $[-1,1]$ while staying responsive: the penalty either left the rails in place or replaced them with a fixed interior level. We read this, as a hypothesis not yet tested, as a sign that the features carry little predictable next-bar signal. A faithfulness verdict has to be recomputed whenever the regime layer or the training recipe changes.
